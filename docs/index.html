PGPO: Prototype-Guided Progressive Obfuscation for Privacy-Preserving LLM-Enhanced Recommendation

Topology-aware semantic obfuscation that protects textual privacy while preserving the relational structure recommendation systems actually need.

Shengxiang Lin1, Jiajie Su1, Pengyang Zhou1, Xiang Chen1, Xiaolin Zheng1,*, Feng Tian2,*, Chaochao Chen1
1 Zhejiang University 2 Xi'an Jiaotong University * Corresponding authors

Overview · TL;DR

Privacy protection should preserve relationships, not just words.

LLM-enhanced recommender systems encode item side information into semantic representations, but those representations can expose sensitive textual attributes through inversion and re-identification attacks. Conventional token-level perturbation can hide content while simultaneously destroying the geometry that downstream recommendation relies on.

PGPO introduces relational fidelity as the central design principle: move each protected representation away from its source while preserving relative semantic structure across items.

Prototype-aware graph

Find structurally influential vocabulary prototypes and precompute semantic neighborhoods.

02

Learn obfuscation anchors that balance individual semantic shift and relational consistency.

03

Progressive expansion

Propagate reliable prototype mappings to the rest of the vocabulary using finalized neighbors.

Method

Prototype-guided progressive obfuscation.

Instead of optimizing every word independently, PGPO first learns a compact set of trusted obfuscation anchors and then expands from the semantic backbone outward.

Build a static semantic graph and identify high-influence prototype words.

Optimization

Three signals, one privacy–utility objective.

R₁

Individual dissimilarity
Push each variant away from its original semantic source.

R₂

Structural consistency
Preserve relative topology among neighboring vocabulary items.

R₃

Semantic constraint
Keep exploration valid and useful rather than arbitrarily destructive.

FAQ

What PGPO is designed to solve.

Why not simply perturb item text or embeddings independently?

Independent perturbation can hide local semantics but may destroy the relative geometry among items. PGPO explicitly optimizes relational fidelity so privacy protection preserves signals needed by downstream recommendation.

Why use prototypes?

High-influence prototype words capture the semantic graph's structural backbone. Learning reliable anchors there first makes subsequent expansion more stable and computationally focused.

What does the repository evaluate?

It includes recommendation backends, semantic and generation-quality analysis, embedding inversion, and white-box source re-identification, enabling privacy and utility to be measured together.

Which datasets are supported?

MovieLens-1M and Amazon-Book are included in the released pipeline.
